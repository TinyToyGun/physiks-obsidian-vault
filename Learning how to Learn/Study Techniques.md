1. The "Concrete -> Symbolic -> Abstract" Cycle
	- Concrete: Visualize or Simulate (Draw Diagrams, Desmos manipulate Variables)
	- Symbolic: Manipulate Algebraically, compute
	- Abstrac: state the principles in words or re-derive from definitions
2. Self-Explanation Training
	1. After every derivation step, articulate in your words why the step is valid. This triggers deep Processing, allowing for better retention.
	2. Write Margin notes: "Because momentum is conserved" "using substitution"
	3. Record Short voice memos (or write atomic "post mortems") 
3. The Feynman Technique: 
	1. Teach a concept as if i was explaining it to a younger student, This highlights gaps in information, and also activates the deep processing part of the brain.
	2. It also highlights the missing links between the intuition and the mathematical formalism
	3. After a section or chapter, write a short note, explaining the concepts extremely simply, or with methaphors etc.. then compare with the textbook, what is missing?
4. Active Recall via "Proof Reconstruction"
	1. Instead of rereading proofs or derivations, reconstruct them from definitions
	2. Cover the proof or derivation, write the statement, and try to get to it with the definitions, if stuck, only uncover the next step, not the whole proof.
5. Spaced interleaving of problem types
	1. Alternate between different but related problems
	2. This is the definition of Depp processing, learning information in context
	3. When doing problems mix chapters
	4. When solving an example pause and reflect which concepts feel similar or are related.
6. Forward-Backward Reasoning:
	1. Forward: Start from assumptions, derivce consequences
	2. Backward: start from the goal, ask "what would i need for this to be true"
	3. Alternate between both until they meet.
7. Definintion chains
	1. Every theorem rests on definitions. train to expand the definitions recursively.
	2. "Continuos" -> $\forall \epsilon \, \exists \delta$ .... -> Geometric meaning
	3. Reduces cognitivie load by anchoring logic to prcies building blocks, and giving meta context to formalism. It allows the formal "boring" part to be imbued with real world context
8. Dimensional Storytelling
	Narrate equations "Force equals momentum change per time; if i scale time by 2, force halves...."
	this forces the deep processing to happen on a dimensional and real world basis, tying the math to physical processes.
9. Extreme-Case & Limiting-Behaviour Checks
	1. For any formula, test its limits: what if x->0 or x to inifinity, what if the mass is infinite or the energy is infinite etc... 
	2. It highlights conceptual errors of especially stuff i do on my own.
10. Dual mode note system:
	1. Splite the notes in two columns:
		- Left: Qualitative concepts, physical meaning, diagrams
		- Right: mathematical derivations
	2. Allows for fast switching between intuition and mathematical formalism.
11. Derivation chains for Memorization-Heavy topics:
	1. Formula dense areas like electromagnetism profit from derivation chains where each formula links to the next. Important is to also highlight why its true arrows, so that the deep processing part of the brain is constantly engaged, and puts the formulas in the web of knowledge.
